The division of labor, stated plainly:

  • The kit guides. It tells you what to do, in order, and gives you the exact words.
  • The AI reads. It takes the paperwork you already have and turns it into answers you can check.
  • You decide. Every call, signature, and payment stays yours.

What an AI service is — in plain words

  • An AI service is a program you type questions to, in ordinary words, and it answers in ordinary words.
  • The useful part for this kit: it can read photographs of documents. Show it a bill, a letter, a form — it reads what’s on the page.
  • It is a reader, not an authority. It can misread, and it can be confidently wrong. The guardrails below exist because of that.

The six moves — the same on every stage

  1. Copy the question. One tap on the stage’s copy button.
  2. Tap the Ask for help button. Her page opens; sign in (first time: make the free account).
  3. Paste. Hold your finger in her question box until Paste appears; tap it.
  4. Add your document pages. Photograph or attach the paperwork the stage names.
  5. Send, and read what comes back against your own documents — the guardrails below say how.
  6. Come back to the stage and do what it says with the answer. The kit’s steps are where answers turn into action.

Before you paste anything — cover what she doesn’t need

Redact first, every time. Some numbers are never hers to see:

  • Never paste: your Social Security number · full bank account numbers · full card numbers · passwords or PINs of any kind.
  • Cover before you photograph: account and member numbers beyond the last few digits, barcodes tied to accounts, anything the question doesn’t ask about. A strip of paper or a thumb over the number before the photo does it.
  • Typed text: replace a number with [covered] before pasting.

The guardrails — true on every stage

  1. The paper wins. If an answer and your document disagree, the document is right and the answer is a misread. Re-check the page yourself.
  2. Deadlines come from paper, never from the AI. Every date that matters is printed on a notice, a bill, or a letter you hold. Read it there.
  3. The AI doesn’t decide anything. Not whether a bill is fair, not whether to sign, not whether to appeal. It reads; you and the kit’s steps decide.
  4. Check the privacy setting once. In the service’s settings, turn off anything that lets your chats be used for training, if the option is offered.
  5. A confident tone is not evidence. These services sound equally sure when they’re right and when they’re wrong. The document check is the difference.
  6. When it matters most, verify at the source. A rule the kit states names where to verify it — the plan, the agency, the notice. The AI is never the source.
