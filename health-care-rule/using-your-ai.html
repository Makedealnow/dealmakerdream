Every stage of this kit hands you paper: plan documents, denial letters, estimates, notices, itemized bills. Reading that paper carefully — every line, against every other line — is the work the whole kit is built on. It is also slow, and the paper is written to be hard to read.

That is the one job an AI service is genuinely good at: taking a document you hand it and giving it back to you in plain words, sorted the way you ask. So the kit’s model is a division of labor, and it never changes:

  • The kit gives you the order — which stage you’re in, what paper to gather, what questions matter, and the exact words (the SAY THIS scripts) for dealing with the humans and the offices.
  • Your AI reads the paper with you — explains it, sorts it, compares two documents line by line, and drafts from facts you give it.
  • You check its answers against the paper, make every decision, and do every act: the calls, the signatures, the filings, the sending.

The AI never talks to your insurer, your doctor, or any agency. It never sees anything you don’t paste. And it is never the authority — the paper is. This page teaches you to use it that way: as a fast reader with no memory you should trust and no knowledge of your plan, leashed to your documents by the exact wording of the prompts the stages hand you.

Read this page once, before you use any prompt in the kit. It is short on purpose. Everything in it protects you.

What an AI service is, in one paragraph

An AI service (also called a chatbot) is a program you type to — or talk to — that answers in plain language. You paste text or upload a photo of a document, ask a question in ordinary words, and it writes back. It is very fast and very confident. It has read enormous amounts of general information, which makes it a good explainer — but it does not know your plan, your program, your state, or your case, and it will sometimes state things that are wrong in the same confident voice as things that are right. Used the kit’s way — fed your actual documents, and forbidden by the prompt’s own words from guessing — it becomes the best reading partner most people have ever had for paperwork. Used carelessly — asked questions from memory, its answers believed unchecked — it is a rumor machine. The difference is entirely in how you use it. That’s what this primer is for.

Sample answer

1. Is it covered? Covered only under conditions. The kit’s working method — six moves, in order

Every prompt in the kit runs on the same six moves. Learn them once; they never change.

  1. The kit tells you what paper to gather. Each prompt names its documents — the SBC pages, the denial letter, the estimate, the notice, the itemized bill. Gather exactly that paper first. No paper, no prompt: an AI answering from memory is the thing this method exists to avoid.
  2. You paste the kit’s prompt. Copy it exactly as written, word for word, into your AI. The wording is the tool — don’t shorten it, and especially don’t cut the last paragraph (the honesty rule below explains why).
  3. You paste your document after it. The text of the letter or bill, or a clear photo of it. Redact first — the rules are in the next section, and they come before any pasting, every time.
  4. The AI answers. Usually in seconds: an explanation, a table, a list of what’s missing, a draft.
  5. You check its answer against the paper. Not against your memory, not against what sounds right — against the document, line by line, the same pencil work the kit teaches everywhere else. Anything in the answer you can’t find in the paper gets treated as not established, whatever the AI’s tone. When the result is a table worth keeping, save it as a photo on your phone, in an album or folder named for your plan — that photo is your record. If a number in the photo ever disagrees with your plan’s own document, the document wins.
  6. You act — using the kit’s words, not the AI’s. The calls, letters, and filings are done by you, with the stage’s SAY THIS scripts and templates. The AI’s output is raw material for your file and your understanding. It is never sent anywhere as-is without your read.

The redaction rules — before you paste anything, every time

Your AI account is yours, but discipline is cheaper than regret. These rules take thirty seconds and they are not optional. This is your rule, applied by you, before anything is shared — you decide what sees. That control is the point; it is not a warning tacked on afterward.

NEVER paste, type, or photograph into any AI service:

  • Your Social Security number — in full or in part. Nothing in this kit’s work ever needs it.
  • Full bank account numbers or full card numbers. A bill’s balance is fine; the account you pay from is not the AI’s business.
  • Passwords — for the AI account itself, your plan portal, your bank, anything. No prompt in this kit will ever ask for one. If anything ever does, it isn’t this kit.

What the work actually needs is smaller than you think: your name is fine (letters are addressed to you, and drafts need it), and dates, amounts, provider names, service descriptions, and the plan’s own reference numbers are the working material of every prompt in the kit. If a document carries a member ID or account number and you’d rather not paste it, black it out and write it back in by hand on anything you later print or send — the AI’s reading loses nothing.

How to black out a number on a photo: on paper, a thick marker over the number, then photograph the page. On a phone photo, open the photo, use the edit/markup tool, and draw a solid box over the number before uploading — and check the edited photo once, zoomed in, to be sure the box is truly opaque. Do this before the photo goes anywhere, not after.

The honesty rule — and why every prompt ends the same way

Read the last paragraph of any prompt in the kit. They all end with the same instruction, in the same words:

Work only from what I pasted. If something is not in the pasted material, write “Not in the document” — do not guess, and do not use outside knowledge to fill the gap. Do not invent numbers, dates, deadlines, or rules. Use plain language.

That paragraph is not decoration. It is the kit’s tradecraft, and here is why it’s there, in plain words:

An AI’s deepest habit is completing the pattern. Ask it about a document and it wants to give you a complete, smooth answer — and when the document doesn’t contain something, its instinct is to fill the hole from general knowledge, or from what documents like yours usually say, without telling you it switched sources. For health coverage, that failure is poison: your plan is not the usual plan, your notice’s deadline is not the usual deadline, and an invented number delivered in a confident voice is worse than no number at all, because you might act on it.

The closing paragraph is the leash. It orders the AI to work only from your paper, to label every gap “Not in the document” instead of filling it, and to keep its hands off numbers, dates, deadlines, and rules it cannot point to in what you pasted. An answer full of “Not in the document” is not a failed answer — it’s a map of exactly what to ask the plan, the office, or the agency next, which is what the kit’s phone scripts are for.

Two corollaries, and then you’re ready:

  • The paper governs. When the AI’s answer and the document disagree, the document wins — always, without a vote. The guardrails below say it again.
  • Never cut the leash to save time. If you retype a prompt from memory and drop the last paragraph, you have built yourself a rumor machine. Copy the prompts whole, every time.

Read this before you trust anything your AI tells you

The guardrails. One page. They protect everything else in this layer. If you read nothing else on this page, read this.

  1. The AI is not a lawyer, and it is not a doctor. It cannot give you legal advice about your case or medical advice about your care, and neither can this kit. What it can do — all it is for, here — is read paperwork you hand it and give it back in plain words. Decisions about treatment belong to you and your doctor. Decisions about lawsuits, court deadlines, and collections actions belong to you and, when those are in play, a lawyer.
  2. Never let the AI decide a deadline. Not an appeal deadline, not a hearing window, not a dispute cutoff. Deadlines in this world are printed — on the denial letter, on the agency notice, on the Medicare notice — and the printed date governs, exactly as every stage of this kit repeats. The kit’s prompts make the AI copy dates out of your documents so you can see them gathered in one place. Copying is all that is. You read the governing date off the paper yourself, you calendar it yourself, and if the AI’s copied date and the paper’s date ever differ, the paper’s date is the deadline. Full stop.
  3. If the AI and the paper disagree, the paper wins — and the kit tells you the move. This will happen. The AI will misread a table, drop a line, or state a rule your documents don’t contain. When it does: believe the document, note the disagreement in your log, and go to the stage you’re in for what to do next — the phone script, the letter, the appeal. The AI’s answer is never the move. The kit’s stage is the move.
  4. The AI does not know your plan. Everything true it says about your coverage comes from the pages you pasted in that conversation. If you start a fresh conversation, it starts empty — paste the paper again. And if you ever ask it a coverage question without pasting, you are back to the rumor machine: general answers about typical plans, delivered confidently, about a plan that is not yours. The kit’s answer to any coverage question is a document. Keep it that way.
  5. Your privacy lives in your AI account’s settings — check them once. This kit never receives your data; MakeDealNow has no window into your AI account and wants none. What your AI service keeps, and whether it uses conversations to train its models, is controlled by that service’s settings — look for its data, privacy, or chat-history controls and set them the way you want them, including any option to keep your chats out of training. The services differ and their settings change; the service’s own help pages are the authority on its own switches. Combined with this page’s redaction rules — no Social Security numbers, no full account or card numbers, no passwords, ever — that is the whole privacy discipline: the kit’s rules govern what you paste, your settings govern what the service keeps.
  6. When in doubt, slow down. Every genuinely dangerous moment in this system involves speed: a deadline invented, a form signed unread, a first bill paid in fear. The AI answers in seconds, which makes slowness feel unnecessary. It isn’t. The paper will still be true in twenty minutes. Check it.

That’s the whole method. The stages still give the order, the scripts still give the words for the humans, and the paper still governs. This page only taught your AI to help carry the file — and now every stage can just hand you the prompt.

Print this and keep it at the front of your file — the redaction rules and the guardrails are worth having on paper.
