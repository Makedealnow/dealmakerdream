Know your plan before anyone bills you.

  • What kind of plan you have — and what's covered, and what isn't.
  • One calm sitting. Card and plan papers in front of you. No bill needed — that's the point.

The steps

Step 1 — Name your plan kind

DO THIS: Find the letters on your card — HMO, PPO, EPO, POS, or high-deductible. Note your kind.

KEEP THIS PAPER: Your card — where you can reach it.

Step 2 — Get the two documents that answer everything

DO THIS: Get your Summary of Benefits and Coverage (the short one — look here first) and your Evidence of Coverage (the full contract). In your plan's app or member site — or one phone call away.

SAY THIS: "Please send me my Summary of Benefits and Coverage and my Evidence of Coverage — the full plan document."

KEEP THIS PAPER: Both documents, in one folder you can find in one minute.

Step 3 — Learn the money words

DO THIS: Pull six numbers from your Summary:

  • Your deductible
  • Your out-of-pocket maximum — your ceiling for the year, the most protective number you own
  • A regular doctor visit cost · a specialist cost · an emergency room cost
  • How your prescriptions are priced

KEEP THIS PAPER: Question 1 below shows all six in one table — save the photo; that photo is your plan card. Prefer paper? The worksheet at the bottom is the same six numbers.

Step 4 — Check a service before you get it

DO THIS: Before any non-routine test, scan, or procedure: look it up, then call the number on the back of your card.

SAY THIS: "I'm scheduled for this service on this date. Is it covered? Is the provider in my network? Does it need approval first? And what will my share be?"

KEEP THIS PAPER: Your note of that call — date, name, reference number, answer. A logged answer is evidence.

Step 5 — Check the network twice

DO THIS: For your main doctor and any specialist, check twice: in the plan's doctor list, and by calling the office — do they take your exact plan? Lists are wrong more often than you'd think; the office knows.

KEEP THIS PAPER: A dated picture or printout of the listing you relied on.

Step 6 — Ask about approval first

DO THIS: Some care needs the plan's OK before it happens. For anything beyond a regular office visit, ask: "Does this need approval first?" If yes — approval in place before the appointment. Write down who told you, and when.

Now do it — two questions, one at a time

  • Question 1 is our lookup — type just your plan's name; it fills in your money words from the official published summary. No AI, no sign-up, nothing personal.

Your plan comes through a job — here's the honest answer

  • Employer plans aren't published in any government file. No website can look yours up — any site that claims it can is guessing.
  • You don't need a lookup. You need your plan's two documents — and you're entitled to both.

DO THIS: Call the member number on the back of your card — or ask HR — and say this:

SAY THIS: "Please send me my Summary of Benefits and Coverage and my Evidence of Coverage — the full plan document."

KEEP THIS PAPER: Both documents, in one folder. Write down who you talked to, and when.

When they arrive: photograph the Summary's cost tables — that photo is your plan card. If the photo and the document disagree, the document wins. Question 2 works from those pages with you.

Medicaid works differently — and this kit has a whole lane for it

  • Medicaid plans aren't in the federal files — there's no honest way to look yours up here.
  • What protects you in Medicaid isn't a price table — it's your rights: written notice before anything changes, a fair hearing if you disagree, coverage that continues while you appeal.

DO THIS: Keep every letter your state sends you. Note your plan's name exactly as it appears on your Medicaid card. Your state's Medicaid agency is the official source for what your plan covers.
