Audible Plans Compared: Monthly, Annual, or Free Trial First?

Audible sells the same membership three ways, and the right one depends entirely on how steadily you listen. Here's the honest comparison — including the plan that's quietly disappearing, and why almost everyone should start with the free trial. The decision, in plain terms

  • Never tried Audible? Start with the free trial. One free book you keep forever, and you'll know in a month whether audiobooks fit your life.
  • Listen in bursts — heavy for a month, nothing for two? Monthly. It's $2.49 more per book than annual, but pausing protects you: pause up to 90 days a year and your credits survive.
  • A book a month, every month? Annual. Same credits, ~$30 a year cheaper, all available up front. Just spend them before they expire — the full math is in our annual plan guide.
  • Two books a month or more? The 24-credit annual brings the per-book cost under $10 — the cheapest Audible gets.
  • Only want to stream, never own? That's what Audible Plus was for — but it's closed to new members now, so the realistic route is a Premium plan and treating the Plus Catalog as a bonus.

The rule underneath every plan: credits are perishable and purchases are permanent. Whichever plan you pick, the winning habit is the same — spend credits on books you'll keep, and never cancel with credits unspent.

Common questions

Can I switch plans later?
Yes — you can change between monthly and annual in your Audible account settings. Many people trial first, go monthly for a few months, then switch to annual once they're sure.

Which plan do most people pick?
Monthly, mostly by default — it's where the free trial lands you. Annual is the better deal only if your listening is steady enough to use all 12 credits inside the year.

Do the plans include the same books?
Yes. A credit buys any premium-selection title on any credit plan, and the Plus Catalog streaming selection is the same while your membership is active.
