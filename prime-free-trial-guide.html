Amazon Prime Free Trial: What You Get, What It Costs After, and How to Cancel

The Prime free trial gives you the full membership — fast delivery, Prime Video, the works — for 30 days. Then it quietly converts to a paid membership on the card on your account. Here's exactly how it works, the two discounted versions most people qualify for without knowing it, and how to cancel in about two minutes while keeping your benefits to the last day of the trial.

How the trial actually works

Sign up and Prime is simply on: free fast delivery, Prime Video, Prime Music, and member pricing, identical to a paid membership. Amazon requires a current, valid credit card on the account to start the trial — that's the card that gets charged later if you forget.

Two things worth knowing before you start:

  • Cancelling early costs you nothing. If you cancel the trial today, your benefits still run to the end of the 30 days. There is no reason to wait until the last day — that's exactly how people forget and get charged.
  • The discounts are real and under-used. Ages 18–24 and students get a 6-month trial and then half price. People on qualifying government assistance (or who verify their income) pay $6.99/month with Prime Access. Check those first if either sounds like you.

The trap: on day 31 the trial automatically upgrades to a paid membership — $14.99 for the month, or $139 at once if you picked the annual plan — charged to the card on your account. Amazon does offer a renewal reminder inside Manage Your Prime Membership (look for the "Remind me" option), and you should still set your own phone reminder for day 25 the moment you sign up.

How to cancel (about 2 minutes)

  1. Go to amazon.com, sign in, and open Account & Lists → Prime Membership.
  2. Choose Manage Membership ("Update, cancel and more").
  3. Choose End membership — during a free trial you may see "Cancel free trial" or "Do not continue" instead.
  4. Amazon will show you what you'll lose and may offer alternatives. Keep choosing to continue cancelling.
  5. Confirm on the final screen ("End on [date]"). Your benefits continue until that date; after it, the membership ends and no charge is made.

Before you cancel: the quick checklist

☐ Use the delivery window. Benefits run to the trial end date even after you cancel — order anything you were planning to order anyway before that date.

☐ Check the final screen's date. It tells you the exact day benefits end. If an option ends benefits immediately instead, pick the one that ends on the trial's last day.

☐ Channels are separate. Any Prime Video add-on channels you subscribed to (Paramount+, etc.) are managed and cancelled separately under Prime Video's subscription settings.

Common questions

If I cancel during the trial, am I charged anything?
No. Cancel before the trial ends and your card is not charged — and your benefits keep working until the trial period ends.

Which is cheaper, monthly or annual?
Annual: $139 once versus $14.99 × 12 = $179.88. Monthly only wins if you'll cancel for several months of the year.

Can I get another free trial later?
Generally, no — not soon. Prime free trials are generally not available if you've been a Prime member within the last 12 months. Amazon confirms your eligibility at sign-up.

I already got charged. Can I get a refund?
Sometimes. Amazon's help pages say paid members who haven't used their Prime benefits are eligible for a full refund of the current membership period. Go to the same Prime Membership page and end the membership — the refund option appears if you qualify.
