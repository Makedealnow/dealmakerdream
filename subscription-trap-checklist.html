The Subscription-Trap Checklist

Almost every "free" trial is a subscription with a delayed start. The trial is real — so is the charge that shows up when it ends, usually for something you stopped thinking about weeks ago. This is the 10-minute sweep that finds yours before they bill you: where each trap hides, what it costs when it springs, and the move that disarms it.

The sweep — go down the list

1. Open Amazon's "Memberships & Subscriptions" page. On Amazon, go to Account → Memberships & Subscriptions. Every Amazon-run trial and membership you have started is listed there with its renewal date and the price it renews at. Anything you don't recognize, cancel on the spot — cancelling a trial usually keeps the benefits running to the end of the trial period anyway.

2. Check Prime itself. A Prime trial converts to $14.99/month or $139/year, and the annual plan is the quiet one — a single $139 charge, once a year, easy to miss and easy to forget you agreed to. If someone in your house is 18–24 or a student, the Young Adults plan is half price after a longer trial — but it can't be shared with a household the way regular Prime can. Details: Prime free trial guide · Prime for Young Adults guide

3. Check Audible — twice. An Audible trial converts to Premium Plus at $14.95/month. Two traps hide here. First, credits: you get 1 credit a month (2 on a first trial if you have Prime), credits expire 12 months after they're issued, and on plans billed through the web, unused credits are lost when you cancel — use them first, then cancel. Second, the pause button: you can pause once a year for up to 90 days instead of cancelling, which keeps your library and stops the charges while you catch up listening. Books you bought with credits are yours to keep either way. Details: Audible free trial guide · Audible plans compared

4. Count your Prime Video channels separately. Prime Video by itself is $8.99/month, and the ad-free upgrade is another $2.99/month. The real trap is channels: each add-on channel you trial through Prime Video bills on its own line, on its own date, even though it all plays in one app. Three forgotten channel trials can cost more than Prime itself. Details: Prime Video guide

5. If you sell or buy for a business, check Amazon Business. Registering a Business account is free — but Business Prime is not: it's billed annually, from $179/year up, and it can be switched on during account setup without the yearly total ever being said out loud. Check which tier, if any, your account is on before it renews. Details: Amazon Business guide

6. Sweep your card and bank statements. Search the last 90 days for small recurring charges — $0.99 to $16.99 is the classic range. App-store subscriptions (Apple or Google Play) bill through the store, not the service, so check both stores' subscription pages too. Any charge you can't name in five seconds gets investigated, not shrugged at.

7. Check the gift memberships. Gift memberships — Audible's, for example — do not auto-renew, so they're the safe way to give a subscription. But if you received one and later started your own paid plan on the same account, both can run at once. Details: Audible gift membership guide

8. Set the reminder the day you start, not the day it ends. Every time you start a trial from today on: before you close the tab, put a reminder on your calendar for two days before it converts, with the post-trial price in the reminder's title. A trial you have to remember is a trap; a trial with a reminder is just free.

The one rule underneath all of it

None of these services hide the charge — they hide the moment. The price after the trial, the renewal date, and the cancel button are all stated on the seller's site; they're just stated once, at signup, when you're least likely to be thinking about them. That's why MakeDealNow's guides state the after-trial price and the cancel steps up front, every time — and why the sweep above works: it takes the moments they spread across a year and puts them all in front of you in ten minutes.
